# Implementation decisions and limitations

## Workflow evaluation

Evaluated [Huey](https://huey.readthedocs.io/en/stable/guide.html), [DBOS](https://docs.dbos.dev/python/programming-guide), and [Temporal](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/python). Huey has SQLite-backed queues, scheduled/periodic execution, retries with exponential backoff, task timeouts and a consumer without an external server. DBOS offers durable function execution; Temporal offers a broader distributed workflow platform. Pipeline does not need a separate orchestration service for a solo operator, so it uses Huey 3.4, with a business outbox and finite preparation checkpoints in SQLite. This is a single-host MVP, not a custom distributed scheduler.

`PREPARATION_DAG` expresses research -> qualification -> drafting; `Pipeline.step` stores node output. Imported Seed evidence is the validated input to research; live discovery uses the public-web adapter before that graph. Long-running conversations use explicit transitions and independent incoming events. Repeated Huey wakeups are safe; business rows and transactional guards are the authority. A crash between business commit and queue dispatch is recovered by the minute outbox sweep.

SQLite serializes mutations at the send/booking boundary. This gives a defined order between a pause/opt-out transaction and an outbound provider request. A recipient reply that has not yet been observed by a successful Gmail poll cannot be acted on; a request already accepted by Gmail cannot be recalled. Polling health needs operator attention. No email exactly-once guarantee is claimed: after a pre-send reservation, uncertain results require reconciliation and never automatic resend.

## Compliance policy, checked October 17, 2026

Sources reviewed:

- [FTC CAN-SPAM business guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business): accurate headers/subjects, clear commercial nature, postal address, usable opt-out and honoring opt-outs. Pipeline suppresses immediately on receipt, earlier than the legal maximum.
- [Gmail sender requirements](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126): sender authentication, low complaint rates and additional requirements for bulk senders. Configure SPF/DKIM/DMARC with the sending-domain provider before activation; Pipeline neither provisions DNS nor evades sending limits.
- [ICO business-to-business guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/) and [CRTC anti-spam guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/anti.htm): recipient/entity distinctions and consent requirements vary. Unknown and non-US regions are blocked rather than treated as US permission.

US is the only implemented policy. The operator reviews each recipient region, source permission and relevance/legal basis; a US domain suffix does not establish location. A published address is not proof of consent or perfect deliverability. The app cannot establish the operator's legal/business facts. Examples contain blank identities so sending fails closed until configured. Robots rules are checked for automatic research; operators still review source terms. Purchased uncertain lists, restricted/logged-in scraping, address guessing and identity rotation are not implemented.

## Gmail and Calendar

[Gmail incremental synchronization](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/sync) uses a persisted historyId, paginated history and ingestion dedupe. Known threads are fetched directly. New threads are matched by In-Reply-To/References metadata before their bodies are read. Delivery-status reports are parsed to find permanent failures and original message IDs. HTML-only messages are converted to text; attachments are not downloaded. Expired history captures a fresh checkpoint, reads tracked threads and scans at most 1,000 metadata records in a 30-day window. Overflow fails with the old checkpoint preserved, requiring operator recovery; messages beyond that window are not guaranteed recovered. API polling is used because no persistent Pub/Sub deployment exists.

[Sending](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/sending) uses MIME plain text and a deterministic RFC Message-ID per outbox message. This is a reconciliation key, not a provider idempotency key. Follow-ups preserve subjects and RFC reply headers according to [Gmail threading rules](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/threads). A 429 can safely retry; uncertain send errors do not. Reconciliation searches sent mail; absence never authorizes an automatic resend.

[Calendar event IDs](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/create-events) are deterministic per contact/start, preventing repeat inserts. The CLI requires a confirmed exact slot with explicit timezone. It rechecks availability under the local mutation gate and records booking state. Google does not offer an atomic free/busy-and-insert operation, so another calendar client can still book the same slot concurrently. Natural-language scheduling is not automatically booked; ambiguous requests escalate.

## Implemented scope versus future work

Implemented: two configurable campaigns; public search and bounded webpage sourcing; quote-backed research; model qualification, drafting, actual installed no-ai-slop editing and final model grounding; explicit review/default dry-run/autonomous gates; Gmail MIME, draft and send adapter, history synchronization, reply processing, cancellation; two business-day follow-ups; Calendar slots and confirmed booking; proposal drafts, project outcomes and feedback, case-study records; per-campaign metrics, stable experiment assignment, suggestion-only optimization; CLI, digest, audit/history, retry/dead-letter/replay; standalone host instructions.

Deliberately manual: sender facts, writing examples, recipient-region/source review, opportunity decisions, confirming meeting agreements, scope/pricing/work commitments, verified customer outcomes and publishing case studies. `win --approve-scope` records an operator-confirmed agreement; it does not send a contract or invent revenue. Proposal drafts are internal and need review. Costs/hours are recorded operator inputs; unrecorded research/model costs are not automatically inferred, so CAC/time totals are partial.

Not implemented: automatic open-ended sales negotiation or autonomous meeting-agreement interpretation; Gmail Pub/Sub; DNS provisioning; paid lead-verification APIs; CRM SaaS synchronization; contract/e-signature/payment processing; rich dashboard; automatic notifications outside the CLI; retention automation; automatic expansion into other jurisdictions; provider-driven adaptive rate tuning. Experiments assign a company once and expose the variant to writing context; statistical significance and optimizer-applied campaign edits are not implemented. Quotes and exact published emails are checked deterministically, but final semantic grounding and style are model judgments requiring real-model acceptance testing. Do not treat mock checks as proof of prose quality or legal compliance.

No production credentials were used. No Gmail messages, drafts or invitations were created during development. No real discovery/model call is claimed. The actual Huey consumer was tested in a subprocess using a digest job. Live provider adapters were exercised through HTTP mocks. Real-account acceptance remains necessary after credentials and explicit campaign approval.
